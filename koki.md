Ingredients
The Base:

2 cups black-eyed peas (or cowpeas) — Note: Peeled black-eyed peas make the process much faster, but unpeeled ones can be used if you peel them yourself.

Fats & Liquids:

1 cup red palm oil (essential for the vibrant orange color and rich taste)

1.5 to 2 cups warm water or vegetable/meat stock

Flavorings & Proteins:

1 cup ground crayfish

Scotch bonnet peppers (to taste, blended or finely chopped)

Salt and bouillon cubes (maggi/seasoning cubes) to taste

Optional: A handful of cocoyam leaves (ndoo) or spinach, finely chopped, or a piece of smoked fish/meat added to the center.

For Wrapping:

Clean banana leaves (or aluminum foil / heat-safe bowls if banana leaves are unavailable)

Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Prepare and Blend the Beans
If using unpeeled black-eyed peas, soak them in water for about 15–20 minutes, rub them between your hands to remove the skins, and rinse them clean. (Skip this if using pre-peeled beans).

Soak the cleaned beans in water for about 1 to 2 hours until they are softened (do not over-soak them into a mush).

Drain the beans and grind them in a food processor or blender with a minimal amount of water until you get a smooth, thick, paste-like batter.

2. Whip and Aerate the Batter
Pour the bean batter into a large mixing bowl.

Using a wooden spoon or your hand, vigorously whip the batter in one direction for about 5 to 10 minutes. This incorporates air, which makes the final Koki light and fluffy rather than dense.

3. Add the Palm Oil and Seasoning
Pour the red palm oil into the whipped batter. As you mix it in, the batter will turn a bright, rich orange color.

Add the ground crayfish, blended scotch bonnet peppers, salt, and bouillon cubes.

Gradually pour in the warm water or stock while stirring, until the mixture has a smooth, pourable consistency (similar to a thick cake batter). Taste the mixture slightly to ensure the salt and seasoning are to your liking.

4. Wrap the Koki
If using traditional banana leaves, pass them gently over an open flame for a few seconds to soften them.

Shape the leaves into small cones or cups, pour a ladleful of the Koki batter inside, and fold the edges securely so no batter leaks out. (Alternatively, you can pour the batter into greased heat-proof bowls or small ramekins covered tightly with foil).

5. Steam the Koki
Place a rack or a layer of leftover banana leaf stems at the bottom of a large pot to create a barrier.

Arrange the wrapped Koki bundles neatly inside the pot.

Pour boiling water into the pot (about halfway up the bundles, ensuring it doesn't spill inside them). Cover tightly with a lid and steam on medium-high heat for about 45 minutes to 1 hour. Check periodically and add more boiling water if the pot starts to dry out.

Once firm to the touch and cooked through, remove them from the heat.
