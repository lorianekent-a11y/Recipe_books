Ingredients
The Greens:

3 cups of finely shredded Okok / Eru leaves (washed and drained)

The Paste & Fats:

1.5 cups of smooth peanut paste (groundnut paste) or natural peanut butter

1 cup red palm oil

Proteins:

1 cup smoked fish (cleaned and deboned)

1 cup smoked meat or cow skin (kanda), pre-boiled until tender

1 cup ground crayfish

Flavorings:

1 medium onion (chopped)

Sugar or a traditional sweetener (to taste — sweet okok is a hallmark of this regional style)

Scotch bonnet peppers (to taste)

Salt and bouillon cubes (maggi/seasoning cubes)

2 to 3 cups of water or meat stock

Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Soften the Okok Leaves
Okok leaves are quite tough and fibrous. To soften them, place the shredded leaves in a pot with a little water (or steam them) for about 10 to 15 minutes until they become tender, then drain any excess water. (Note: Some people prefer to cook them directly in the sauce, but pre-softening speeds up the process).

2. Prepare the Peanut Base
In a separate, large pot, mix the peanut paste with about 2 to 3 cups of water or meat stock until smooth.

Bring it to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn at the bottom. Let it cook for 10 minutes until the raw peanut flavor disappears.

3. Add the Meats and Palm Oil
Into the simmering peanut base, add your pre-boiled smoked meat/kanda, smoked fish, and chopped onions.

Pour in the red palm oil. Stir well and let the mixture bubble together for about 5 minutes.

4. Combine Everything
Add the softened Okok leaves into the pot.

Stir in the ground crayfish and scotch bonnet peppers.

Season with salt and bouillon cubes.

The Secret Touch: Add a tablespoon or two of sugar (adjust to your preference) to give it that signature sweet-and-savory Okok flavor.

5. Simmer to Perfection
Reduce the heat to low, cover partially, and let the entire mixture simmer for 15 to 20 minutes, stirring frequently.

The sauce should thicken into a rich, cohesive, oily paste where the peanut sauce completely coats the shredded leaves.
